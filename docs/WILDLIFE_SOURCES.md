# Wildlife content notes

## Important limitation

Wildwood Trail is a fictional decision game, **not emergency advice or certified survival training**. Tigers are dangerous and their responses depend on context. Never seek a close encounter or enter prohibited areas to practise game choices. Follow current instructions from authorised local guides and forest officials. The tiger's circular movement and the 15-second deadline are fictional gameplay, not biological claims.

## Reference material

1. **Wild Tiger Health Centre — How to respond if you encounter a tiger.** The information sheet distinguishes different encounter types, advises against turning your back and running, discusses slow controlled retreat in suitable circumstances, not following the animal, cub-related caution, and reporting encounters. The quiz deliberately avoids teaching one universal eye-contact/noise rule because the sheet differentiates situations. The document's searchable extract was consulted; the direct host presented a challenge page during retrieval. [2](https://wildtigerhealthcentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/WTHC_Info_sheet-How_to_respond_to_meeting_a_tiger.pdf)

2. **Pench Tiger Reserve, Maharashtra — FAQ / visitor rules.** The reserve requires authorised guides; its rules say visitors should remain in vehicles except at authorised places, give wildlife passage, avoid leaning out, shouting, music, and littering. Reserve-specific distance/time figures were deliberately not turned into universal quiz facts. [5](https://penchtigerreserve.maharashtra.gov.in/faq/)

3. **National Tiger Conservation Authority — Guideline compendium.** This describes regulating tourism to minimise disturbance and avoid risky close encounters. Local regulations and current directions remain more relevant to a particular visit than a general game. [1](https://ntca.gov.in/assets/uploads/guidelines/Guideline_compendium.pdf)

## Editorial approach

The 36 questions are newly authored scenarios, not verbatim quotations or an official examination. Some are conservative extensions of avoidance, noninterference, and obeying authorities (for example: not handling an injured wild animal, respecting closures, or refusing a risky selfie). No organisation has endorsed or certified this game.

- Close encounter questions distinguish a tiger that is not charging from a distant safari sighting.
- Quiet observation advice is restricted to undisturbed safari viewing; it is not a universal emergency rule.
- No question recommends provoking, feeding, handling, chasing, fighting, playing dead, or climbing a tree as a guaranteed way to handle a tiger encounter.
- The general theme is avoidance, distance, controlled actions when appropriate, and trained local help—not winning a confrontation.
- The user controls one explorer for game simplicity. This does not imply that walking alone in real tiger habitat is permitted or advisable.

Before deploying this as a public educational product, obtain a review from a qualified local wildlife professional and update the content for the specific reserve/region. Sources and regulations can change.


## Version 2 curriculum and languages

The age-group profiles are self-selected question styles; they do not assess ability from age. Difficulty is selected independently. English, Hindi and Marathi prompts and explanations are authored offline. The 24 shared scenarios include simple recognition, risk comparisons, and evidence-based reasoning; 12 additional scenarios personalise the context. Have local wildlife professionals and native-language editors review content before public use. Route branches are fictional authorised options, not directions for visiting a real reserve.
